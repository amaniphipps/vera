# Enablement kit

A kit gives a customer's admin everything they need to launch one feature internally, so they never start comms from a blank page. Structure and branding stay the same on every kit. Two things change per delivery: the account context and the feature profile.

## Inputs

You need two things: the **account** and the **feature**. Everything else is looked up. Do not interview the requester step by step. Make reasonable assumptions, build the kit, and list assumptions in the reply or the open-items section.

## Section order

| # | Heading | What goes in it | Varies by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How to use this kit | Who it is for, what is inside, the feature in one paragraph, where the account stands today | Account + feature |
| 2 | Recommended rollout plan | Numbered phases with owners: admin test, pick owner and channels, soft launch, open up and announce, launch week, keep it going | Account timing |
| 3 | Admin setup checklist | Where the setting lives, checklist with already-done items ticked | Feature |
| 4 | Announcement copy | Copy boxes: in-app announcement, email with subject and preview, channel posts; add a note for restricted access | Feature + account words |
| 5 | Manager talking points | What it is, why it helps them, what it costs, moments to use it, a script for a team meeting | Feature |
| 6 | Employee quick-start | Short numbered steps per role, link to help docs | Feature |
| 7 | Use cases | Shape set by the feature: occasions table, moments list, or first-things-to-try | Feature + account |
| 8 | Employee FAQ | 8 to 10 questions answered from docs, in the account's currency word | Feature |
| 9 | Open items and resources | Checklist of unconfirmed items, then doc links | Both |

Drop a section only when it truly does not apply, for example quick-start roles for an admin-only feature.

## Workflow

1. **Gather account context.** From notes and memory: launch owners, current settings, concerns raised, their internal word for the currency, related programs, timing, and anything already promised.
2. **Pin product truth.** Re-check every fact against current docs before it goes into the kit. What was demoed but is not in the docs goes into open items, not into customer copy.
3. **Write the content spec.** Fill the nine sections in order. Customizable bits go in brackets.
4. **Render.** Use the house output format from `brand-design`. Keep headings real and styles consistent.
5. **Verify.** Render to an image and look at it. Check the logo, title, headers and footers, boxes, and tables.
6. **Deliver, then report** in one or two sentences: one line on account-specific assumptions, and the count of open items.

## Guardrails

- Customer-facing, so every claim must be true.
- Keep internal-only material out: pricing disputes, risk signals, renewal talk, other customers' names.
- Copy meant for the customer's employees should sound like that customer's own team wrote it, not like a vendor ad.
