# Audience tuning

The same feature is described three ways. This reference sets the depth and word choice per reader, and pins down the idiom the enablement skills use to keep audiences separated.

## The three registers

| Register | Reader | Their question | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive | Leadership | What changes, and by how much? | So-what first, numbers only, one page |
| Practitioner | Operator | What do I click, and what are the limits? | Steps, field names, limits, where it lives |
| Customer | End user | What do I get, and why should I care? | Benefit first, plain language |

## Word choice by register

| Concept | Executive | Practitioner | Customer |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new capability | "cuts review time in half" | "two fields and a toggle in Settings" | "you finish in one step" |
| A setting | "turned on company-wide" | "Admin > Features > Enable" | "your admin turns it on" |
| A limitation | "one open risk: scale" | "50-character title limit" | "works for teams up to 100 people" |

## Rules

1. **Keep one audience per document.** Do not blend registers in the same section. If a section opens for an executive, every sentence in it stays executive.
2. **Match depth to the reader, not the feature's complexity.** A simple feature still gets exact inputs for a practitioner and outcome language for an executive.
3. **Put mechanism where practitioners look.** FAQs and setup sections hold the how. Value lines stay about the outcome.
4. **Use the customer's own words.** Write customer copy as if that customer's team wrote it. Their currency word, their value names, their program names. Not the vendor's.
5. **Flag, do not fill.** An unknown applies at every register, stated one sentence in the reader's register.

## Verbosity instinct

When a request does not name an audience, infer it from the requester's job, then write to the deepest register and cut upward. A full kit is built to the practitioner register; the exec summary on top is the same content reduced to outcomes. A demo one-pager is built for the rep (practitioner register), but the copy blocks inside are written for the buyer's employees (customer register). Keep those two layers separate rather than blending them.
