# Demo setup one-pager

The rep's job on demo day is to build the instance, paste, and present. This output does the thinking before that: what the buyer cares about, what the agreed positioning says to show, and what exact inputs make the instance feel like theirs. The deliverable is one short page, a fixed set of sections, every value with a copy button.

## Output sections

1. **Create the account.** Fields in the order the form presents them, the admin rows, then anyone to invite after. Each person gets a one-line why.
2. **Account information.** Values validated on the buyer's own site, a copy block of their values, and a logos table with links.
3. **First actions.** One or two starter items written to sit at the top of the instance, each mirroring the work of someone being invited.
4. **Feature focus.** The one or two features chosen. For each: why (tied to what the buyer said), what peers do (with numbers), and what to update (exact inputs, copyable).

Header: account name, who it is for, date, and the deal link. Footer: sources and how peer data was pulled. No pricing, risks, talk tracks, or timeline.

## Workflow

Research runs before any feature decision. Skipping ahead produces a generic page.

1. **Understand the deal.** Read for: deal state (first demo, re-demo, or win-back), the incumbent or alternative, what the buyer reacted to, their success definition, their tools and structure, and the named people.
2. **Research the account.** Their site for values and logo, recent news, team pages for real titles and emails, and public principles. Validate values against the buyer's own site, never an aggregator.
3. **Decide what to spotlight.** Order the signals: what the buyer asked for or reacted to, what their workforce shape calls for, where the alternative is weak, and any recent news. Pick one or two; two is the ceiling.
4. **Back each feature with peer data.** Compare against similar accounts. Report honestly: "none of the peers use it" is a finding.
5. **Choose who to invite.** Fill the required roles in order: the decider, the helper the buyer named, an executive who buys on outcomes. One line each on why.
6. **Write the first actions.** Mirror the work of an invitee, anchor on something real, use only the account's own values. Role-based placeholders, never real names.
7. **Verify, build, publish.** Confirm every field limit before stating it. Copy the reference template, keep its CSS and copy-button script, replace all content.

## Voice

Plain and direct. Every line either gives the rep something to paste or tells them why it is there. Flag unknowns in one sentence, never with filler.
